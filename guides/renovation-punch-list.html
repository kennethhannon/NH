How to document renovation defects with a usable punch list

Turn scattered photos and complaints into a clear list that a contractor can actually resolve.

Describe the location precisely

Start with room, wall or floor area, and a repeatable physical reference. 'Floor bad' does not help a crew locate the issue; 'north edge of living room, third board from sliding door, 12-inch finish streak' is actionable.

Write one issue per distinct defect. A broad complaint combining ten problems is difficult to track and verify, especially after some repairs are finished and others remain open.

Use evidence that shows scale

Take a wide shot for context and a close-up for detail. Include a ruler, straightedge or reference object when scale matters. Photograph finishes under normal lighting and again under raking light if the defect is visible only at a certain angle.

Keep the original photos outside the browser. An online punch-list tool may resize images for convenience, but it should not be your only archive of project evidence.

Separate priority from cost

A cosmetic paint touch-up and a potential water intrusion issue deserve different urgency. Rank the safety and functional problems first, then use consistent priority labels for remaining defects. Cost estimates should be clearly labeled preliminary.

Different trades might require a repair, removal and replacement, or a reinspection; avoid diagnosing a technical failure without evidence or qualified review.

Track closure instead of just reporting

For each issue, track when it was reported, who is responsible, what corrective work was agreed, and whether the result was inspected. A resolved label should mean the owner actually accepted the finished work—not simply that the contractor says it is done.

Download a backup of the list and produce a dated PDF before a walkthrough. Treat the report as a project-management record, not an expert certification or legal determination.
