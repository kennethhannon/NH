How to compare a total-loss vehicle settlement

A practical worksheet for separating the insurance offer, replacement costs, outstanding loan, and comparable vehicles.

Start with four different numbers

The insurer's proposed vehicle value is not the same as the cash you receive after deductibles, and neither equals the cost of replacing your car. Keep them on separate lines. A payout can also be sent partly or entirely to a lender. The amount of money left after loan payoff may be negative.

For example, imagine a proposed value of $27,000, a $1,000 deductible, and a $26,500 loan balance. An illustrative $26,000 net check would leave a $500 loan shortfall before any other reimbursement or gap coverage. That does not tell you what an insurer legally owes.

Build a realistic replacement budget

Collect actual listings with the same trim, mileage, equipment, condition and geographic market where possible. Record each listing's date and source. An asking price is not a completed sale price, so use it as research rather than proof of market value.

Add estimated sales/use tax, registration, title and any likely dealer fee to your personal replacement budget. These expenses can be useful for planning, but whether they must be paid by an insurer depends on policy, state law, liability and the facts of the claim.

Watch out for double counting

Do not subtract the deductible twice. If an initial settlement amount is already net of a deductible, subtracting it again will understate the check. Likewise, if the insurer's figure already includes taxes or fees, do not add the same amounts again.

Keep a spreadsheet or exported estimate showing which amounts come from the insurer's documents and which are your own assumptions. Ask for a written valuation report and a line-by-line explanation of any adjustments you question.

Prepare useful evidence

Record the vehicle identification, trim, factory and paid software equipment, maintenance condition, mileage, and supporting listings. Save screenshots or PDFs because public advertisements can disappear. Flag substantially different vehicles rather than averaging them without adjustment.

A calculator helps organize a negotiation; it cannot determine liability, market value with professional precision, or state-required reimbursements. For a disputed claim, review your applicable policy and local claims rules.
